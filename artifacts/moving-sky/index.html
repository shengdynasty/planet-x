SPHEREx Moving Sky

Compare two passes of the infrared sky and find what moved.

Simulated field

SPHEREx re-maps the whole sky every six months. Asteroids, comets, brown dwarfs and any undiscovered planet show up only as things that change position between images. Pick a later pass to compare with the first, blink them, and the detector flags every source that moved, appeared, vanished or brightened — then checks each one against the known-object ephemeris.

    What is simulated here and what is not

    Simulated: the star field, the noise, the band response, and three planted objects (a bright main-belt asteroid on a known orbit, a variable star, and one faint, slow, uncatalogued mover at roughly 500 AU — the kind of signal a Planet X would leave). Nothing here is a real detection.

    Taken from the real mission: the 6.15″ pixel scale, bursts of four exposures ~2.4 minutes apart followed by a revisit hours to a day later, the ~6-month survey cadence, the rule that frames can only be compared at matching wavelength (SPHEREx’s linear variable filters mean wavelength changes across the detector), and the detect → match → ephemeris-check logic.

    To run on real data, replace the frame generator with cutouts from NASA/IPAC IRSA (SIA collection spherex_qr2, 60–120″ around a target) and the ephemeris stub with JPL Horizons and the small-body identification service. Every other step stays the same.
