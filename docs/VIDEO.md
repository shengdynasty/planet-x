# Demo video: shot list (30 seconds)

Recorded on the hosted app, https://shengdynasty.github.io/planet-x/app/, in Advanced mode, desktop browser at
1440×900 or larger, light or dark theme. Every shot uses saved real data, so nothing depends on a server or on IRSA.
Narration is about 75 words: read it at a calm pace, one line per shot.

| Time | Shot (what to do on screen) | Narration |
|---|---|---|
| 0–4 s | Sky view, zoomed out, the badge reading *Saved SPHEREx data* in view. Drag slowly. | Planet X searches NASA's SPHEREx sky survey for the one thing a distant planet can't hide: parallax. |
| 4–9 s | Scroll-zoom into the Milky Way toward the galactic centre until the 64k tiles are sharp. | SPHEREx sees every patch from opposite sides of Earth's orbit, so a distant body jumps while the stars stay put. |
| 9–15 s | Field view, Examples → 16 Psyche. Blink, then Difference; click the Known object. | For any patch, the app blinks and subtracts every pass at matching wavelength. Here, asteroid 16 Psyche, matched to JPL. |
| 15–21 s | Examples → Epsilon Indi Ba/Bb. In *SPHEREx spectrum*, press **Field centre**; hold on the plot. | Spectra on the fly: a brown dwarf pair measured in 300 exposures, its methane dip and four-micron peak. |
| 21–26 s | Press **Ask**, then the chip *What can't it find?* (or type *Can it find Planet Nine?*). | And it's honest: a Planet Nine-class world is below SPHEREx's single-exposure depth, and it says so. |
| 26–30 s | Back on the sky; end card or caption with the app link and github.com/shengdynasty/planet-x. | Candidates for people to vet, never claims. Open source, in your browser. |

Before recording: reload the page once (warm the tiles), close other tabs, and turn off notifications. If the spectrum
shot is slow, open the Epsilon Indi field first so its saved spectrum is cached. Keep the cursor still while narrating.
Upload as unlisted on YouTube (or the team drive), check it plays signed out, then put the link in docs/PROJECT-PAGE.md,
README, the write-up's Links and the pitch outline's last slide.
