Find the planet that barely moves.

SPHEREx maps the whole sky every six months. A distant Planet X would drift only slightly between those passes. Planet X compares the passes, flags what moved, and rules out the asteroids and comets we already know.

The problem

One image can't tell a far planet from a star. Two can.

An undiscovered planet hundreds of AU from the Sun would be faint and nearly still. In any single exposure it looks exactly like a background star.

Repeat the same patch of sky months apart and it gives itself away: the stars stay put, the planet shifts. The hard part is everything else that also changes, including asteroids, comets, nearby brown dwarfs, variable stars and detector glitches, and leaving an honest short list of what is still unexplained.

How it works

From raw exposures to a short list worth a second look.

The same pipeline runs on simulated fields in the browser and on real SPHEREx images through the local server.

  1. Collect

    Pull every SPHEREx exposure covering a patch of sky from NASA/IPAC IRSA and cut out the same field from each epoch.

  2. Align and match

    Pair epochs at the same wavelength. SPHEREx's filters vary across the detector, so mismatched pairs would show colour, not motion.

  3. Subtract and detect

    Subtract the epochs, keep sources above 5σ, reject single-pixel spikes that aren't star-shaped, and pair what vanished with what appeared.

  4. Cross-match and review

    Check each mover against JPL's known asteroids and comets. What's left goes to a person to mark for follow-up or reject.

The app

Three views, one review queue.

Everything runs in the browser with no install. Pick a mover anywhere and follow it from the whole sky down to the pixels.

Sky

The whole sky, every mover on it.

Drag around NASA's Deep Star Maps, jump to coordinates or a candidate number, and step through the scans or blink between them.

  • Tracks join each mover's positions across scans
  • Filter, sort and export the Movers list

Compare

Two scans, side by side.

Drag a wipe across two epochs of the selected mover, switch to a difference image where anything that moved shows up twice, or blink them.

Field

Run the search on one patch of sky.

Choose a reference pass and a comparison, then see the detection pipeline's flags: uncatalogued movers, variables, known objects and artifacts.

  • Blink, difference and track (max-stack) modes
  • Add any flag to the review queue

Data

Real sources, clearly labelled.

The badge in the app's top bar always says where the pixels came from.
